01 / THE IDEA

What if a story
was a place to explore?

Stories Beyond the Screen explores how artists used the early internet to invent new ways of telling stories. In a book or a film, audiences usually follow a fixed order. In these artworks, however, the visitor helps shape the experience: clicking an image, choosing a link, or opening a digital window reveals another piece of the story.

The three pieces in this exhibition were created between 1996 and 2000, when artists were experimenting with the web as a creative medium rather than simply a place to share information. Olia Lialina uses splitting browser frames to reveal a difficult conversation; Shelley Jackson turns the human body into a map of memories; and Yael Kanarek builds a fictional world inside a computer-like interface.

Although their subjects differ, all three use the structure of the webpage itself as part of the storytelling. Their fragmented paths encourage curiosity and make each visitor's journey a little different. Together, the works show how early net artists transformed clicking and navigation into artistic choices—and made the viewer an active participant instead of a passive reader.

A note for visitors: the artwork links lead to independent websites or archives. Some older projects may work differently in modern browsers.

01 — CONVERSATION / FRAGMENTS

My Boyfriend Came
Back from the War

Olia Lialina • 1996

A couple reunites after a war, but their conversation unfolds in pieces. As viewers click the images and words, the screen splits into smaller frames, slowly revealing the tension between the characters.

02 — BODY / MEMORY

my body —
a Wunderkammer

Shelley Jackson • 1997

An illustrated body becomes a map of personal memories. Clicking different body parts opens stories and reflections, allowing visitors to discover a life through details rather than a traditional beginning-to-end narrative.

03 — JOURNEY / DIGITAL WORLD

World of Awe:
The Traveler's Journal

Yael Kanarek • 2000 (Rhizome presentation date)

Visitors explore an imagined landscape through a computer-like interface. Journal entries, images, and love letters reveal the experiences of a traveler searching through a mysterious world.
